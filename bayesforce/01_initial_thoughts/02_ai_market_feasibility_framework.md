> **Core Question:** *Given the structural cartography of the AI market, what kinds of businesses are realistically buildable by Bayesforce—matching our capital, capabilities, distribution channels, risk tolerance, and service-to-product trajectory?*

## I. The Three-Layer Evaluation Architecture

To avoid premature conclusions or mixing unrelated variables, our feasibility framework explicitly separates **Market Quality**, **Startup Execution Mechanics**, and **Bayesforce Strategic Fit**:

```text
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ 1. MARKET ATTRACTIVENESS (Is this an intrinsically great problem to solve?)    │
│    • Market Pain • Economic Value • AI Leverage • Market Structure           │
└──────────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────┘
                                       ▼
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ 2. STARTUP FEASIBILITY (Can an early-stage startup realistically win here?)  │
│    • Platform/Incumbent Risk • Distribution Difficulty • Sales Velocity      │
│    • Implementation Complexity • Retention & Moat • Capital & Time to Revenue│
└──────────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────┘
                                       ▼
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ 3. BAYESFORCE ADVANTAGE (Is this the right business for US to build?)        │
│    • Service→Product Potential • Founder Distribution Edge                   │
│    • Founder Learning Flywheel • Operating Archetype Alignment               │
└──────────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────┘
                                       ▼
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ DECISION STATUS: [Investigate] / [Validate] / [Shortlist] / [Park] / [Reject] │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## II. Strategic Evaluation Dimensions

Every sub-segment is evaluated across fourteen explicit criteria grouped into the three core layers:

### Layer A: Market Attractiveness (The Problem Space)
1. **Market Pain:** Is the problem acute, urgent, and frequently experienced, or merely an annoyance?
2. **Economic Value:** Is there substantial, identifiable budget already tied to this problem (e.g. expensive payroll, revenue loss, margin leakage)?
3. **AI Leverage:** Does AI provide a 10x structural leap in speed, cost, or quality, or merely an incremental 15% automation?
4. **Market Structure:** Are buyers fragmented and numerous (high pricing power, multiple at-bats), or concentrated among a handful of oligopolistic buyers?
5. **Competitive Density:** How crowded is the venture/startup landscape (measured empirically by YC company count and market entrants)?

### Layer B: Startup Feasibility (The Execution Mechanics)
6. **Platform & Incumbent Risk:** Can frontier foundation model labs (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google) or platform giants (Microsoft, Salesforce, ServiceNow, Adobe, Shopify, vertical ERPs) absorb this feature natively?
7. **Distribution Difficulty:** Can a startup reach and acquire target buyers cost-effectively, or is distribution blocked by closed networks?
8. **Sales Cycle Velocity:** How fast can a deal close (e.g. 14–30 days for operational mid-market vs. 9–18 months for enterprise RFPs)?
9. **Implementation Complexity:** How difficult is deployment (API integration & human workflow setup vs. custom on-prem hardware/telemetry)?
10. **Retention & Moat Potential:** Once deployed, can the system become deeply embedded through workflow integration, data accumulation, and operational trust?
11. **Capital Requirement:** How much upfront capital is required before proving unit economics?
12. **Time to First Revenue:** How fast can the business generate its first ₹1L / ₹10L / ₹1Cr (e.g. Day 30 via diagnostic/service vs. Month 18 post-R&D)?

### Layer C: Bayesforce Fit (The Unfair Advantage)
13. **Service → Product Potential:** Can we initially sell the *outcome* as an operational engagement, learn the messy ground-truth workflow, and progressively productize the repeatable agentic software?
14. **Founder Distribution Edge & Learning Flywheel:** Do we have direct access to buyers, geographic proximity, and does running the engagement generate proprietary workflow insights that compound our advantage?

## III. Status & Decision Taxonomy

To prevent premature bias or false precision, sub-segments begin in `[TBD]` state and transition through rigorous discovery:

- `[TBD]` — Baseline scaffold ready for evidence collection and constraint filtering.
- `[INVESTIGATE]` — High potential on paper; requires secondary research and competitor deconstruction.
- `[VALIDATE]` — Active discovery; conducting founder interviews, buyer outreach, and problem validation.
- `[SHORTLIST]` — Passed all hurdle criteria; primary candidate for initial beachhead pilot.
- `[PARK]` — Viable and attractive market, but timing, capital, or founder edge is not currently aligned.
- `[REJECT]` — Structurally unfeasible for Bayesforce (e.g., custom silicon fabs, pure B2C novelty, multi-year government procurement).
